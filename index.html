Autopay Exit

Subscriptions and loans quietly pull money through UPI AutoPay mandates. Keep your mandates in one list, follow the documented exit path for each, and track every attempt until the status reads cancelled. This page organises and explains - it cannot cancel anything for you, and no cancellation is guaranteed by anything here.

How a UPI AutoPay mandate actually ends

Stopping the money is not ending the agreement behind it. Revoking a mandate stops the automatic pull - it does not cancel what the payments were for. A subscription you no longer want still needs cancelling with the merchant, or dues can pile up. A loan EMI is different: a loan cannot simply be ended - tell the lender and agree how you will repay instead, because a missed EMI can mean late fees, penal interest and a credit-score hit. And check timing before you rely on a revocation: if a debit date is close, confirm with the app or bank when the stop actually takes effect.

Route 1 - inside the UPI app that created it

Open the UPI app where the mandate was created, go to Profile or Settings → AutoPay / Mandates / Subscriptions, open the active mandate, tap Cancel or Revoke, and confirm - usually with your UPI PIN (RBI's rules require an extra authentication step at revocation). NPCI confirms that modify, revoke, pause and unpause are standard features inside UPI apps.

Sources: npci.org.in/product/autopay and HDFC Bank's mandate-cancellation guide hdfc.bank.in/payments/services/cancel-upi-autopay-mandate - both read 23 Nov 2026 and re-checked 25 Nov 2026.
Not every mandate can be stopped in the app. HDFC Bank's guide says that, as per the regulator, mandates for some merchants (for example debt collection and loan repayment) cannot be cancelled through the UPI app, and you should contact the merchant or lender directly. If the Cancel option is missing or greyed out, that is the likely reason - use Route 2 or Route 3 and do not assume the payment has stopped.
Timing: NPCI says customers get a pre-debit notification at least 24 hours before a debit, and HDFC Bank suggests cancelling at least 2-3 days before the next scheduled payment. A revocation made just before a debit date may not stop that debit - check the status afterwards and keep your proof.
Honest limit: menu names and screens differ between apps and change over time. These are the general documented steps, not screenshots of your exact app version. If you cannot find the mandate, look for "Autopay", "Mandates", "Subscriptions" or "UPI Mandate" under your profile.

Route 2 - from the merchant's side

Some mandates can also be cancelled on the merchant's own app or website (for example, ending the subscription itself). After doing that, open your UPI app and check that the mandate status shows Cancelled or Revoked - ending a subscription does not always end the payment mandate behind it.

Source: HDFC Bank guide, read 23 Nov 2026 (same link as above).

Route 3 - through your bank

If the app routes fail, contact your bank - its app, net banking, helpline or branch - and ask what mandate-revocation route it offers for your account. RBI's e-mandate rules treat revocation as your action protected by an extra authentication step (AFA), but how each bank lets you exercise that varies, so the honest instruction is to ask them directly rather than follow a generic path. Keep your mandate list and attempt log from this page handy for that conversation.

Source: RBI circular on e-mandates in UPI, 10 Jan 2020: rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Notification.aspx?Id=11784 - read 23 Nov 2026.

Three money truths

Cancelling stops future debits only. Amounts already debited are not automatically refunded - a refund is a separate ask to the merchant. Pause vs revoke: pause when you will want the mandate back, revoke when you are done. Verify, don't assume: after any cancellation, the mandate's status in the UPI app should read Cancelled or Revoked - a vanished subscription tile is not proof.
Sources: HDFC Bank guide and NPCI page linked above, read 23 Nov 2026.
